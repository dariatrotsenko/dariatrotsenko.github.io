Daria Trotsenko

Theoretical physicist
Ph.D. student, Harvard University

I am a Ph.D. student in theoretical physics at Harvard University, where I work on particle physics with Matthew Schwartz. I study the strong interaction — quantum chromodynamics — and how it shows up at particle colliders. Most of my work uses effective field theories and precision perturbative calculations to understand what happens when hadrons collide and break apart.

Right now I am working on forward neutron production at electron–proton colliders, developing a factorization framework for the process together with Stella Schindler (Los Alamos National Laboratory). Before coming to Harvard I did my B.Sc. and M.Sc. in Amsterdam, with a master's thesis at Nikhef on the analytic calculation of Drell–Yan angular coefficients at NNLO. I grew up in Ukraine and started my physics studies at Taras Shevchenko National University of Kyiv. More details are on the research page.
